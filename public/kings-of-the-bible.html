The kings category of the Bible begins and ends with Him: the Lion of the tribe of Judah (Genesis 49:10; Revelation 5:5), the Son of David (Matthew 1:1), the Alpha and the Omega (Revelation 22:13), enthroned at the right hand of God (Psalm 110:1; Hebrews 1:3), who returns on a white horse to judge and make war in righteousness (Revelation 19:11-16). Every earthly throne below is a footnote to this one.

The Last King — The Beast & the Tribe of Dan Conjecture

The throne line closes with its darkest mirror: a king who is not a king, the Beast who receives worship that belongs to the King of Kings. Historically, and by many Bible researchers, it is assumed that the Antichrist will somehow spring from the tribe of Dan — a conjecture built from a striking omission, a serpent prophecy, and the unanimous voice of the earliest Fathers. This scriptorium documents it as conjecture: Scripture alone binds; the Fathers' opinion and the companion blog research are filed beneath it.

The Biblical File on Dan

Genesis 49:17

The Serpent by the Road

“Dan shall be a serpent by the way, an adder in the path, that biteth the horse heels…” — Jacob's dying oracle paints Dan in the very image Scripture later gives the serpent, Satan (Revelation 12:9).

Deuteronomy 33:22

The Lion's Whelp

“Dan is a lion's whelp: he shall leap from Bashan.” — the same lion imagery given to Judah (Gen 49:9), from whom Christ sprang; Hippolytus reads it as the tribe of the counterfeit lion.

Jeremiah 8:16

The Snorting from Dan

“The snorting of his horses was heard from Dan… he shall also come and devour the earth.” — Irenaeus reads this as the Antichrist's tribal marker.

Revelation 7:4-8

The Omission

In every extant Greek manuscript (Sinaiticus ℵ, Alexandrinus A, P47), Dan is absent from the sealed 144,000 — the pattern is deliberate, not a copyist's error.

Judges 18 · 1 Kings 12

The Idolatrous Record

Dan carried a graven image to Laish “all the time the house of God was in Shiloh” (Judg 18:30-31), and received Jeroboam's golden calf — “Behold your gods, O Israel” (1 Ki 12:28-30); Amos 8:14 records oaths “as thy god liveth, O Dan.”

Tel Dan

The Spade Confirms

Excavations at Tel Dan uncovered the massive high-place platform matching 1 Kings 12's cultic site, and the 9th-century Tel Dan Stele — archaeology physically confirming the tribe's idolatrous reputation.

The Fathers & the Researchers

c. AD 180

Irenaeus — Against Heresies V.30

“Jeremiah… even indicates the tribe from which he shall come: ‘We shall hear the voice of his swift horses from Dan…' This, too, is the reason that this tribe is not reckoned in the Apocalypse along with those which are saved.”

c. AD 200

Hippolytus — On Christ & Antichrist §14

“As the Christ was born of the tribe of Judah, so will the Antichrist be born of the tribe of Dan… Let Dan be a serpent, lying upon the ground, biting the horse's heel.” — the typological mirror image.

3rd c. & Second Temple

Victorinus · Testament of Dan · Sibylline Oracles

Victorinus on Rev 7: Dan “chosen to be a judge of the people, fell into a serpent's path.” The Testament of Dan says Dan's future prince is animated by deceit and “his prince is Satan”; Sibylline Oracles 2.170-175 count only ten tribes in the end times.

Theological Inquiries

Why does the kings category start with Jesus?

Because He is the true head of every throne line: the Lion of Judah, the Son of David, King of Kings and Lord of Lords (Rev 19:16), the Alpha and the Omega (Rev 22:13). All earthly reigns — Saul to Nero — are footnotes to His throne, and He returns to judge the world in righteousness (Acts 17:31; Matt 25:31-46).

Is the Beast really from the tribe of Dan?

The Bible never says so explicitly. The conjecture rests on Dan's omission from Revelation 7 in every Greek manuscript, Genesis 49:17, Deuteronomy 33:22, Jeremiah 8:16, and the explicit testimony of Irenaeus and Hippolytus. Jerome and many modern scholars call it speculation; alternative readings point to Rome/Nero or a Gentile ruler of the fourth beast. The scriptorium documents it as the historic majority conjecture, under Scripture's silence.

What happens to the last king?

The Beast is taken and “cast alive into the lake of fire burning with brimstone” at the appearing of the King of Kings (Revelation 19:20), destroyed “with the brightness of his coming” (2 Thessalonians 2:8).

Are the apocryphal kings part of the canon?

No. The Seleucid and Hasmonean tables are labeled Apocrypha and cited by chapter only; 1 Maccabees itself testifies that prophecy had ceased in Israel by that era (1 Macc 9:27).
